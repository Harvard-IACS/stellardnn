Estimating physical parameters of M87*

Tao Tsui and colleagues

Computer vision models perform well at tasks such as image classification and object recognition. In this project, we train neural networks on general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations to infer physical parameters of the supermassive black hole M87*. We explore multitask learning, masked models, long short-term memory networks, attention layers, and super-resolution imaging.

Generating M87* images with adversarial networks

Arya Mohan and colleagues

Simulations of M87* are expensive to run, which limits the size of the training grid. We use conditional progressive generative adversarial networks to generate synthetic black hole images across a range of spin and electron distribution parameters.

Reconstructing M87* images with diffusion models

Yuqing Pan and colleagues

InstructPix2Pix-M87 is a multi-conditional diffusion model that improves the quality of Event Horizon Telescope images. The model trains on GRMHD simulations of M87*. This approach improved deblurring and parameter inference in the project’s tests.

Estimating M87* parameters from visibility data

Franc O and colleagues

The Event Horizon Telescope imaged the supermassive black holes M87* and Sgr A*. We investigate how neural networks can estimate M87* parameters directly from visibility data in the frequency domain. We trained models on standard and normal evolution (SANE) and magnetically arrested disk (MAD) simulations. Static and dynamic models performed well for SANE. Dynamic or polarimetric data were needed for accurate results in the MAD simulations. We also applied the approach to real M87* data.
